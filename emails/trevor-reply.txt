You're not struggling to find decision makers. Sales Navigator and
Dripify do that fine. What's missing is a filter on the COMPANY before
you spend a meeting on it — so qualification happens in the room, which
is the most expensive place it could possibly happen. Right order is:
score the company on energy first, then go find the person inside it.

I've built you a skill that does exactly that. Install it in Claude and
it'll score a company out of 100 off industry, shift pattern, equipment
and size, estimate the monthly spend, and tell you go or no-go. Works on
one company or on a list of four hundred.

Download (don't unzip it):

In Claude: your name bottom-left, Settings, Customize, Skills, upload
the zip. Then start a NEW chat and say "score this company for me" with

Three things in there worth saying up front.

1. The first thing it'll do is make you state a number. Your pitch is
that you find more waste than the system costs — so there's a minimum
monthly electricity spend below which the deal can't work, whatever
anyone wants. Rental divided by the waste you typically find, divided
again by what clients actually act on. Until that's a rand figure,
"large energy user" is a feeling and every list is a guess.

2. The best qualifying question in the country is the tariff name.
Megaflex applies above 1 MVA of notified demand, so "we're on Megaflex"
IS the qualification — no estimating needed. People read it off the bill
without feeling interrogated, because it's admin, not commercial. Follow
with "what's your NMD in kVA?" Ask both before you book anything and
most of the wasted meetings disappear.

Worth knowing too: NMD is contracted, not measured, and you're billed on
the higher of notified or actual. So plenty of sites are paying every
month for capacity they never touch and nobody's noticed. That's a
finding your system produces in month one, and it's a better opener than
any percentage.

3. Your free Gap Analysis Report is a good lead magnet and you're
spending it too early. Gate it on the tariff and the NMD rather than on
a meeting. It's obviously necessary information, so it reads as
competence, not screening — and anyone who answers has qualified
themselves.

One more thing, and check it with your accountant before you use it.
Section 12L was due to lapse end of 2025 and Treasury extended it to
31 December 2030, at up to 95c per kWh saved, with M&V by a SANAS-
accredited body. Your system is the measurement layer those claims need.
That turns a 60-month rental from a cost against savings into a cost
against savings plus a deduction, which is a materially different
conversation.
