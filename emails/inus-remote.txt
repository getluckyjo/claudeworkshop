Workbook attached. It's the workshop, rebuilt for doing on your own —
the whole sequence written out rather than the short version I'd hand
someone with me standing next to them.

Set aside about three hours, or three sittings of an hour. It works
either way.

Two things before you start, and both take twenty minutes the night
before rather than at the top of the session:

1. Paid Claude plan, then install the desktop app rather than just the
browser. If your card declines it's your bank blocking the international
payment — enable that in your banking app.

2. Add the skill, don't unzip it:
https://github.com/getluckyjo/claudeworkshop/raw/HEAD/dist/entrepreneurcoach.zip

In Claude: your name bottom-left, Settings, Customize, Skills, upload the
zip. Then open a NEW chat and say "help me get set up". Skills don't load
into a conversation that's already running, which catches everyone once.

On the Shopify side, since the design and build is the part you care
about. "Build a Shopify site" is three jobs. The store setup — payments,
domain, shipping — is you in the admin and always will be. The products
are a CSV and an afternoon, and you've already said that's the easy half.
The theme is the third one, and it's the one people assume is off the
table.

It isn't. A theme is a folder of files, and Shopify has a GitHub app that
keeps a repo branch and a theme in sync both ways. Put Claude on that
repo and the loop is: ask for a change, look at it, merge it, see it in
the preview. No terminal anywhere in that.

The bit worth reading twice is in the workbook: don't ask it for a page,
ask it for a section with a schema block. A page is a fixed thing only
Claude can change. A section with settings shows up in your theme editor
with its own controls, and you design with it afterwards without asking
anyone. You're not commissioning pages, you're building yourself a
control panel.

One rule: work on a duplicate theme, never the live one. Connect the
GitHub branch to the duplicate. Get that wrong and every merge is a
deployment to customers.

One steer on scope. Aim to finish with the loop working and one section
you built and then adjusted yourself in the editor. That's the moment it
clicks. A whole storefront is the week after and it goes quickly once
the loop exists. The way this goes wrong is perfecting the hero before
the loop works — get something ugly moving first.

Bring the brand: logo, fonts, colours, whatever art direction exists.
And three sites you'd be proud to have made plus one you can't stand.
The one you hate is worth more than the three you like.

Shout if anything fights you. I'd rather hear about it while you're in
it than afterwards.
