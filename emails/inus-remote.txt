Workbook attached. It's the workshop, rebuilt for doing on your own —
the whole sequence written out rather than the short version I'd hand

Set aside about three hours, or three sittings of an hour. It works

Two things before you start, and both take twenty minutes the night
before rather than at the top of the session:

1. Paid Claude plan, then install the desktop app rather than just the
browser. If your card declines it's your bank blocking the international
payment — enable that in your banking app.

2. Add the skill, don't unzip it:
https://github.com/getluckyjo/claudeworkshop/raw/HEAD/dist/entrepreneurcoach.zip

In Claude: your name bottom-left, Settings, Customize, Skills, upload the
zip. Then open a NEW chat and say "help me get set up". Skills don't load
into a conversation that's already running, which catches everyone once.

On the Shopify side, so you know where the line falls. It won't build
the store — theme, payments, domain, navigation is you in the admin.
Products are a different matter, and there are two routes. The workbook
lays both out; short version is it can hand you a product import CSV you
review and import yourself, or it can write straight into the store over
the Admin API if someone sets up a token.

I'd do the launch on the CSV. Not because the other one doesn't work,
but because getting products in takes minutes either way and writing
forty descriptions that sound like the brand takes an afternoon on both.
What the spreadsheet buys you is reading all forty before anything
exists in a store with the group's name on it.

Either way, the copy is the point: product descriptions, SEO fields,
policy pages, welcome emails. That's the part that actually costs a
launch three weeks.

One steer. Step 1 is writing the brand's voice down, and it looks like
the soft one. It isn't. Do the range before the voice and you get forty
products that read like a template, and re-doing them costs more than
doing them slowly once. You'll want to skip ahead. Don't.

Bring three or four things already written in the new brand's voice —
even a packaging back-panel or a caption. That's what decides whether the
output sounds like the brand or like a stock coffee company. Some Platō
copy is useful too, so it can hear what to move away from.

Shout if anything fights you. I'd rather hear about it while you're in
it than afterwards.
