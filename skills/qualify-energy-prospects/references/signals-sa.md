# What's observable in South Africa

Signals that can be checked from a desk, roughly in order of how much they tell
you per minute spent.

## Tariff, and why it's the best question

Eskom's **Megaflex** is a time-of-use tariff for customers billed directly by
Eskom with a **notified maximum demand above 1 MVA**. So the answer "we're on
Megaflex" is close to a complete qualification on its own — it means Eskom has
contracted over a megavolt-ampere of capacity to that site.

The useful ladder, largest first:

- **Megaflex** — Eskom-direct, NMD above 1 MVA. Large. Pursue.
- **Miniflex** — Eskom-direct, smaller urban demand. Mid. Worth asking more.
- **Nightsave** — urban or rural, demand-based. Mid, and the name itself says
  they already shift load, so somebody there thinks about energy.
- **Ruraflex** — rural time-of-use. Often agriculture, pack houses, irrigation.
  Can be substantial; check pumping.
- **Municipal large power user** — most metros have an LPU or high-demand
  category, usually with a notified demand and a kVA charge. Name varies by
  metro, so ask what the tariff is called on the bill rather than guessing.
- **Business or commercial tariff, no demand charge** — small. Decline.

**Ask for the name on the bill.** People read it off; they don't estimate it.

Two things to understand about demand charges, because they are the opening for
the whole conversation:

**Notified maximum demand is contracted, not measured.** The customer notifies a
figure, Eskom accepts it, and billing works off the higher of that figure or the
actual measured peak. So a company carrying an NMD well above what it uses is
paying every month for capacity it never touches — and nobody notices without
metering. That is a finding Trevor's system can produce in the first month, and
it is the most persuasive opening he has.

**Exceeding the NMD is penalised.** A site that trips over its notified demand
on a few winter afternoons is paying for that across network capacity charges.
Fifteen minutes of avoidable coincident load can cost more than a month of
lighting, which is precisely the kind of thing granular data catches and a
monthly bill does not.

Rates change every April. Never quote a tariff figure from memory — check the
current Eskom Schedule of Standard Prices.

## Shift pattern, without asking

- **Job ads.** The most reliable public signal there is. A company recruiting
  shift fitters, boiler operators, refrigeration technicians or "nightshift
  supervisor" has just told you the shift pattern and half the equipment list.
  Worth watching continuously, not just when researching one company.
- **Google reviews and opening hours.** Delivery and collection windows often
  reveal when the plant actually runs.
- **Gate traffic in Street View**, where the imagery is recent enough to be
  worth anything.

## Satellite and Street View

Twenty seconds each, and better than most desk research:

- **Roof area** — measure it. Floor area is the input the estimate needs.
- **Cooling towers, condenser banks, chimneys, extraction stacks** — visible
  from above and each one is a load.
- **Solar already installed** — changes the pitch rather than ending it. They
  have already decided energy matters and spent money on it. The question
  becomes what the panels aren't covering, and whether anyone is measuring it.
- **Standby generation and large fuel tanks** — a site that invested in backup
  has load it cannot afford to lose.
- **Truck bays and reefer points** — cold chain.

## Public disclosure

- **JSE-listed companies** publish sustainability or integrated reports, and
  many disclose electricity consumption in kWh or GJ and Scope 1 and 2 carbon.
  That is the actual answer, published, for free. Always check before guessing
  — and note it names the group, not the site.
- **Carbon tax registrants and large emitters.** Being liable means fuel
  combustion at scale. Thresholds and rules sit with Treasury and SARS and have
  moved more than once, so check current guidance rather than quoting a number.
- **B-BBEE and supplier scorecards.** Companies supplying large retailers or
  automotive OEMs are increasingly pushed on energy and carbon by their
  customers. That pressure is a better reason to buy than the saving, because it
  has a deadline attached and the saving doesn't.
- **Section 12L.** Extended by National Treasury to **31 December 2030**, at up
  to **95c per kWh saved**, with measurement and verification by a
  SANAS-accredited body. Relevant twice over: it tells you a company has done
  the work, and it's a reason the rental partly pays for itself. Tax rules move
  — confirm before putting a figure in a proposal, and send them to their own
  accountant.

## Sectors worth building a list around

Where the waste tends to be concentrated, and the pitch is easiest to make
specific:

- **Cold storage, abattoirs, dairies, ice.** Refrigeration runs continuously, it
  degrades quietly, and defrost and setpoint waste is invisible without
  measurement. The clearest single sector.
- **Plastics — injection moulding and extrusion.** Heater bands, barrel
  insulation, drying, chillers, and a per-part energy cost the company almost
  never has. Trevor's per-machine costing angle lands harder here than anywhere.
- **Industrial laundries.** Continuous, hot, pumped, and run on thin margins.
- **Foundries, galvanising, heat treatment.** Enormous loads, and usually
  already paying attention, which makes it a shorter conversation.
- **Poultry, piggeries, controlled-atmosphere storage.** Ventilation and heating
  around the clock, and often on Ruraflex.
- **Hospitals and private clinics.** 24/7, heavy HVAC, sterilisation, and a
  facilities team that reports upward on cost.
- **Hotels and resorts.** Seasonal, HVAC-heavy, with a measurable gap between
  occupancy and consumption that nobody is currently measuring.

## Where not to spend time

Offices, professional services, agencies, insurers, dry retail, schools, light
assembly, warehousing without refrigeration. Not because they waste nothing —
they do — but because the waste is too small to beat a 60-month rental, and
every hour there is an hour not spent on a cold store.

**Writing them off properly is worth as much as finding good ones.** A list of
decided nos with reasons is what stops the same dead company coming round again
next quarter looking new.
