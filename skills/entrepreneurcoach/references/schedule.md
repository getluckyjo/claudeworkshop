# Station 5 — Make it repeat

**This is a sold deliverable.** The workshop page promises "one job running on a
schedule." Don't let the session end without it.

## The pitch

Everything so far still needs them to show up and ask. This is the first thing
that happens without them.

> Up to now you've had to open Claude and ask. This one runs on its own — Monday
> at seven, the brief is waiting for you.

For an owner who named a recurring task as the thing eating their week, this is
the station where the money is. Give it the time.

## What to schedule

**Use their pre-work answer.** They already told Johannes which recurring task
eats the most time and creates the least value. That's the job. Don't run a
fresh discovery — read it back and confirm.

The pattern that works: **something that reads, then reports.** A schedule is
most useful when it goes and looks at real data and hands back something they'd
actually read.

| They said | Schedule this |
|---|---|
| I never know where the money is | Monday 07:00 — cash position, who owes what, anything overdue since last week |
| Enquiries go cold | Daily 08:00 — who emailed yesterday and hasn't had a reply |
| I chase my team for numbers | Friday 16:00 — the week's numbers, and who hasn't submitted |
| Month-end swallows a week | Monthly — assemble the pack from source and flag what moved |
| My diary runs me | Sunday 18:00 — the week ahead, where the gaps are, what needs prep |

**The rule: it must produce something they'd read.** A scheduled task that
generates a wall of text nobody opens is worse than nothing — it teaches them
automation is noise. Short, specific, and it should tell them what to *do*.

## Setting it up

**Lead with the conversational route.** They don't need to find a menu:

> Ask for it in plain language — "every Monday at 7am, check Xero and send me
> the cash position and anything overdue." That's the whole setup.

Claude creates the recurring task from the description. No forms, no cron, no
settings screen. For a beginner this is far and away the easiest path.

There's also a **Routines / Tasks** area in the sidebar where they can see what's
scheduled, edit it, pause it or delete it. Show them where it lives so they don't
feel they've set something loose they can't stop — that fear is real and it stops
people using it.

> ⚠️ **Facilitator: check the exact path on your own build the week before.**
> Scheduled tasks exist across the Claude app, Cowork and Claude Code, the
> surfaces differ, and this one moves faster than any other feature in this
> flow. The conversational route is the stable one — teach that, and treat the
> menu as a bonus.

**Needs a paid plan.** Pro or Max, which the workshop already requires. Tasks run
in the cloud, so their laptop can be shut.

## Then fire it once

**Do not leave it untested.** A schedule nobody has seen run is a promise, not a
deliverable.

Run it immediately, on the spot, and show them the output. Then fix what's wrong
with it — there's always something, usually that it's too long or reports things
they don't care about. Trim it in front of them.

That's also the lesson: it's *theirs*, and they change it by asking.

## Say these out loud

1. **It runs whether or not you're thinking about it.** That's the entire point,
   and it's the first time Claude has done anything without them.
2. **It can't do anything you haven't allowed.** It reads what they've connected
   and reports back. Sending and changing still get asked about.
3. **They can pause or delete it in seconds**, from the same place they see it.
4. **Start with one.** Someone who schedules six things on day one reads none of
   them by Friday. One good Monday brief beats a full calendar of noise.

## Snags

**No option to schedule anything**
Free plan. Scheduled tasks need Pro or Max. The workshop requires a paid plan
anyway, so check they're signed into the right account — people have a personal
free login and a paid work one and land on the wrong one.

**It ran but the output is empty or generic**
Almost always that the task can't reach the data. The connector it depends on
has to be available to the scheduled run, not just to the chat they set it up
in. Re-check the connector, then fire it again.

**It's too long and they won't read it**
The most common real failure, and it kills the habit within a fortnight. Rewrite
the prompt to ask for a short answer with a clear action. "Three lines and what I
should do about it" works better than any amount of formatting instruction.

**They're nervous about it running unattended**
Fair. Show them the pause button before they leave, and start with something
read-only. Trust builds once they've had two or three useful Mondays.
