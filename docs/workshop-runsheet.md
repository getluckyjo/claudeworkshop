**Claude for entrepreneurs.** 10:00–13:00, CHIPS, 21 Roodehek Street, Gardens, Cape Town. 8 seats,
R3,500. Facilitator notes — not for the cohort.

Assume: **owners and executives, no code, no terminal, on the desktop app.**
They've done an hour of pre-work and a paid plan is already active.

## What was sold

Six things, running, before they leave. This is the contract — protect all six:

1. A Project that knows their business
2. Live data — Workspace, M365 or Xero
3. One real artefact, built in the room
4. A Skill that repeats their process
5. One job running on a schedule
6. 14-day call and 30-day review (booked in the room)

If time runs short, cut **depth**. Never cut one of the six to zero — that's the
thing they'd be entitled to complain about.

**The station numbers are the skill's solo order, not the room order.** Someone
running it alone at home goes 0→6 in sequence; in the room you follow the blocks
above. The skill handles being taken out of order — just name the station.

**Block 8 carries two stations in fifteen minutes.** It's the tightest block on
the sheet and the one most likely to overrun. Two ways to protect it: have them
build the skill *from what just worked* in block 7 rather than starting fresh,
and set the schedule conversationally rather than through the menu. If it's
going badly, the schedule matters more than the skill — the skill is easier to
finish alone afterwards.

## Prep from the eight answers

The single highest-leverage hour you'll spend. You have all eight before the day,
so arrive with a page per person:

- The job they named, in their words
- Which connector it needs — Workspace, M365 or Xero
- What the artefact should be — spreadsheet, deck, document, dashboard, page
- What the schedule should be, and when it should fire
- The one thing likeliest to block them

Then share those answers with Claude before the session and run each one through
independently — you'll find the two that don't work as stated before you're
standing in front of the person, not after.

Eight jobs on the whiteboard in block 2 should be *their* eight, already written.
That's the moment the room realises the session was built around them.

## What will actually go wrong

**One or two arrive without the skill installed.** Expected — that's what block 1
is for. Have the no-file fallback ready: new chat, ask Claude to create the
skill, paste the content in. Don't hold the room; peer help catches the rest.

**Wrong Google account.** Half the room has personal and business Gmail. Call it
out early and save yourself ten minutes.

**Microsoft people hit an admin wall.** Personal Microsoft accounts can't connect
at all; write tools are gated behind the tenant admin. The read-only demo still
lands. Note it as homework and keep moving.

**Connector pop-ups blocked.** Common on locked-down work laptops. Incognito
usually fixes it.

**Vercel signup wobbles.** Free Hobby plan, personal account. If someone stalls,
switch them to an artifact — or to a file. Do not let a signup form cost someone
the best moment of the day.

**The schedule produces a wall of text.** The most common real failure and it
kills the habit inside a fortnight. Trim it in the room: "three lines and what I
should do about it."

**Someone races ahead.** Second artefact, or have them help a neighbour. Peer
help scales better than you do.

**Someone falls behind.** Get them to block 7. That's the one that makes the day
worth the ticket.

## The one thing that matters

If someone leaves with nothing else, they should leave with **a real artefact
they changed themselves** — a model that redid its own five years when they
changed one assumption, or a live page they edited by asking. That's the moment
it clicks, when "AI writes code" becomes "I can do this myself."

Protect block 7. Take the time out of block 3 if you have to.
