#### What the PnP file actually is

Columns that matter: `SOH Qty` (stock on hand), `DROS Qty` (daily rate of sale),

#### The trap that would have wrecked the build live

**`Days Cover = 0` does not mean out of stock.** 1,017 rows show zero days cover.
Only **181 of them have zero stock**. The other **834 are stores holding stock
that simply isn't selling** — days cover is zero because the *sales rate* is
zero, not the stock.

Sort on days cover, as anyone would, and you get a list that's 82% wrong. Find
this at 12:10 on Saturday and the block is gone.

**The mirror image is worse.** Days cover runs up to **30,377 days** — Hyper
Bloemfontein sitting on 2,339 units of Teazy Wild Berry against a sales rate of
0.077/day. 1,085 rows show over a year's cover. Those aren't well-stocked
stores, they're dead stock, and they're invisible in any report sorted the
obvious way.

**So the rule to teach him: never read Days Cover without reading DROS beside
it.** That one sentence is worth the ticket.

#### The filter he has to decide, not us

557 of 6,022 rows should come out before any ranking:

- **74 distribution centre rows** — not stores. They'd top every league table
- **186 delisted lines** (`Listing Status = X`)

Clean active set: **5,465 rows.** Ask him to confirm which statuses count as
active — it's his call and it changes every number downstream.

#### The Checkers trap, and it cuts both ways

Each article appears once per **sellable pack** — single can, 4-pack, 24-pack —
and again per purchasing org. Patch Margarita, latest week:

| Pack | Units | Sales |
|---|---|---|
| 150ML CAN (single) | 1,005 | R43,687 |
| 4X150ML CAN | 2,303 | **R320,308** |
| 24X150ML CAN | 13 | R10,513 |

**Those are three different products on shelf with their own sales.** So:

- **Rand values must be summed across the pack rows.** Drop the multipacks — the
  obvious "de-duplicate" move — and you lose 88% of Margarita's revenue.
- **Units must not be summed.** One row counts cans, the next counts 4-packs.

- **Store counts must not be summed.** The same 359 stores repeat on every pack

**This is the trap that catches people in both directions**, and it's why the
prototype in `prep/checkers/` reconciles its totals to the file's own vendor
total sheet — R803,732.61 core, R954,647.47 Patch, to the cent. Any method that
doesn't reconcile is wrong, and that check takes ten seconds.

### Liezl — Cape of Storms Apothecary *(remote)*

**Not attending in person.** She gets the workbook and runs the build herself.

**No longer the unknown in the room.** Johannes has consulted on this business
and already has the direction, so her session doesn't depend on her reply.

**The direction:** move from selling individual apothecary products to a
**R1,000/month subscription box, Cape Town only.** 1,000 subscribers is R1m a
month, R12m a year, off a single metro — and it's far cheaper to produce, sell
and deliver than scattered one-off orders.

**Cape Town-only is the smart half of that**, and worth saying to her explicitly.
1,000 boxes a month in one metro is roughly 45 drops a working day — routable,
own-vehicle territory, no national courier margin. Delivery cost per box falls
with density, which is exactly what a subscription needs and exactly what
national distribution destroys.

#### The build: her subscription model

Not a positioning page any more. She has the strategy; what she doesn't have is
the answer to *does it work, and what does it take.*

**What it holds:**

- Box price (R1,000) and what's in it
- Cost to make one box — ingredients, packaging, labour
- Delivery cost per drop, and how that falls as density rises
- **Churn**, monthly
- Acquisition cost per subscriber
- Fixed monthly costs

**What it tells her, live, as she changes an assumption:** contribution per box,
how many subscribers cover fixed costs, and what the ramp to 1,000 actually
demands.

#### The number worth putting in front of her

1,000 subscribers is a target. Here is the same thing as an operation:

**At 5% monthly churn she loses 50 subscribers a month at steady state.** To
reach 1,000 inside a year from a standing start, she needs roughly **110 new
subscribers every month — about 25 a week, every week, for a year** — because
she's replacing churn the whole way up.

That's not an argument against the strategy. It's the thing the strategy needs
to be built around, and it's invisible in "1,000 × R1,000". A subscription
business is an acquisition machine that happens to deliver boxes. If the model
shows her one number on the day, show her that one.

And it cuts the other way too: **drop churn from 5% to 3% and the machine she
needs shrinks by about a third.** For an R&D founder that reframes the job —
retention is a product problem, which is the part she's actually good at.

#### What her shop actually sells — confirmed 18 Aug

Two collections, Face & Body and Home. Confirmed price points: **Deodorant R95**,
**Charcoal & Tea Tree Soap R70**. Face Oil (Squalane & Argan) and Oxygen Bleach
are in the range but their prices weren't indexed. **Free shipping over R600.**
Positioning is "less is more" — no petrochemicals, synthetics, perfumes or
preservatives, often only two or three ingredients.

**And they run workshops** — formulation sessions, one-on-one and group, in their
own studio at the Old Castle Brewery.

#### The tension — do NOT tell her this

Her products are R70–R95. A R1,000 monthly box is **10 to 14 units**. No
household uses twelve bars of soap and deodorant a month.

That is the single most useful thing she can discover on Saturday, and it is
worthless if she hears it from you. **Let step 1 below produce it.** She will
build the box, reach six items and R500, and stop. That pause is the session.

If she hasn't hit it by about minute 8, prompt — don't answer: *"how far are you
from a thousand?"*

#### How she builds it — the 35 minutes

**1. Build the box (5 min).** Her real products at her real prices, until it adds
up to R1,000.

> "I'm putting together a R1,000 monthly subscription box. Here's what I sell and
> what it costs — help me fill the box."

She hits the wall here. Sit with it. Don't rescue it.

**2. Cost it (5 min).** What each item costs her to make, plus packaging and the
drop.

> "Now add what each one costs me to make, and show me what's left per box."

Contribution per box appears. First real number she owns.

**3. The engine (10 min).** This is where 1,000 stops being a target and becomes
an operation.

> "If 5% of subscribers cancel every month, how many new ones do I need each
> month to reach 1,000 within a year?"

She gets **~110 a month, about 25 a week, every week.** She computes it; nobody
tells her. Then the counterweight, which is the hopeful half:

> "What if churn were 3% instead?"

The machine shrinks by roughly a third. **Retention is a product problem** — and
product is what she is actually good at. That reframe is the moment worth
protecting in this whole block.

**4. Break it on purpose (10 min).** Her Station 3 "change one thing" moment,
four times over:

> - "What if the box were R750?"
> - "What if it went out every second month instead?"
> - "What if I only had four items but they were R250 each?"
> - **"What could go in the box that isn't a product?"**

**That last question is the one to make sure she reaches.** She runs workshops in
her own studio. She will get there herself — and a subscription that includes a
monthly workshop slot is a far easier R1,000 sell than twelve products, because
it monetises an asset she already owns. **If you say "workshops" before she does,
you have taken her insight.** Ask the question, then wait.

**5. Name the answer (5 min).** Which version does she actually believe? That is
her commitment for Station 6.

#### What she leaves with

Not a plan she was given — a plan she stress-tested. If she changes the price to
R750 and watches breakeven move, she owns the number. If she is told R1,000
works, she owns nothing.

- **Artefact:** spreadsheet. Six inputs she can change, not sixty.
- **Connector:** Drive or Gmail, low stakes. Hers is the shortest connector stop.
- **Skill:** the monthly subscriber review — what came in, what churned, where
  the run rate to 1,000 stands.
- **Schedule:** monthly, not weekly. Subscription numbers don't move weekly and a
  weekly report would teach her to ignore it.
- **Watch for — and this is the real risk:** she's being handed a strategy she
  didn't build. Everything above is designed so she reaches the findings herself.
  The temptation to shortcut it will be strong, because you already know the
  answers. Resist it — the value is in her arriving, not in her being told.
- **The call is now optional.** Worth ten minutes if she offers, but nothing in
  the session depends on it.
